How Scheduler works

Scheduler is a date poll. You offer some dates, share one link, everyone marks which ones they can make, and the best date highlights itself. Here is every step, including the parts most people only find when they need them.

Create a poll No account needed, no ads.

1. Create the poll

Give it a title, then choose dates. There are several ways to do that, and they combine:

  • Click days on the calendar. Click again to remove one, or shift click to add a run of days. Days already past cannot be picked.
  • Use a preset: the next 7 days, weekdays next week, or the next 4 Fridays.
  • Repeat certain weekdays across a date range, such as every Tuesday and Thursday between two dates, in one step.

Then choose times. "All day" asks only about dates. Time slots ask about times as well: set opening hours, such as 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, and a slot length from 15 minutes to 4 hours, and every chosen day gets those slots. Add a different window for certain days, such as shorter hours on Saturday, and on those days it replaces the everyday hours. Tap any single slot in the list to switch it off.

Last, decide whether people can answer "maybe" as well as yes and no. It is off by default. Optionally add your name, some details, and a voting deadline. A poll can offer up to 300 dates and times.

2. Two links: one to share, one to keep

Creating the poll gives you two links. The share link is for everyone. The organizer link is for you: anyone holding it can change, close or delete the poll. Its secret sits after a #, the part of a link browsers never send to a server, so it stays out of server logs.

This browser remembers the organizer link, but nothing else does unless you save it. You can copy it, download both links as a file, or have both emailed to you. That address is used for that one email and not stored. If you would rather not type it in at all, open the links in your own mail app instead.

3. Everyone votes

People open the share link, type a name and tap yes or no on each option, or maybe if you allowed it. There is no account, no email address and no app. Options are grouped in one card per day, and a shortcut line sets an answer for a whole weekday or start time at once. Tapping an answer you already gave clears it.

Timed options show on each voter's own clock, with a switch to see them as the organizer entered them. All day dates never move. A poll holds up to 60 people.

Voters can change their answers later from the same browser. Everyone can also leave comments, and edit their own comments later the same way.

4. The best date picks itself

Each yes counts two points and each maybe one. The highest scoring date or time is highlighted in the grid and named above it, along with how many people said yes. When options tie they are all shown, and when lots of them are level the page names the earliest instead of listing them all. "Add to calendar" downloads a calendar file for the winner.

5. What the organizer link can do

  • Close voting, and reopen it later.
  • Turn "maybe" on or off, even after people have answered.
  • Add a date, with or without a time, or remove one. Removing a date also removes the answers given on it, and the page warns you first.
  • Remove anyone's row or comment.
  • Delete the whole poll.

If you set a deadline, voting closes on its own at that time and the poll page shows everyone how long is left.

6. Your polls on this device

The home page lists the polls this browser created or opened with an organizer link, so you can find them again without digging through messages. That list lives only in this browser. Clearing site data removes it.

7. Signing in, if you want to

Accounts are optional and unlock nothing that a poll needs. Signing in keeps your polls on any device. Enter your email address and Scheduler sends a sign in link that works once and expires after 15 minutes. Polls you create while signed in appear on your My polls page. Open that page in a browser that already holds organizer links, and those polls are added to your account too, unless another account has them already.

Who can see a poll

Polls are private by link, not by password. Anyone with the share link can see the title, details, dates, every name and answer, and the comments. Poll pages are kept out of search engines. The privacy page lists everything Scheduler stores.
