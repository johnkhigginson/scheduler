A Crab Fit alternative for when you have a shortlist

Crab Fit is a free, open source tool built around a heat map. You give a rough range of dates, everyone clicks and drags across a grid to mark when they are free, and the overlap shows up as the darkest cells. Scheduler starts from the other end: you offer specific dates or time slots, and each person answers yes or no to each one.

Why a yes or no list instead of a grid

Dragging across a grid is quick with a mouse, and it is a good way to say "I am free all of Tuesday afternoon". It is harder on a small touch screen, where a drag can scroll the page instead of marking cells. Scheduler shows one card per day with large yes and no buttons, so a voter on a phone taps through the list. A shortcut line sets a whole weekday or time at once, such as "set no for every Monday", for polls with many slots.

The results read differently too. A heat map shows where availability overlaps. A tally answers a narrower question: of these options, which one can the most people make? Scheduler highlights that option, shows ties as ties, and lets anyone download it as a calendar file.

When Crab Fit is the better choice

Pick Crab Fit when you do not have candidate dates yet and want to see where everyone's free hours fall across a week, or when you are looking for a standing weekly time rather than one date, which its days of the week mode answers directly. It is also the one to choose if you want an Android app or want to host the software yourself.
