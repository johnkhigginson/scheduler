Find a family reunion weekend everyone can make

A family reunion gets planned around the least flexible people in the family: a cousin who works weekends, grandparents who will not fly in winter, school calendars in three different districts. The people answering range from teenagers to great aunts who have never made an account for anything, and the date has to be settled early enough to book a venue.

Set it up

  1. Start four to eight months out. Under "Or repeat certain weekdays across a date range", pick Saturday, plus Sunday if it is a two day gathering, set the range across the season you have in mind, and choose Add those days. One step adds every weekend in it.
  2. Keep it all day. All day dates never shift with time zones, so the relatives three states away see exactly the Saturday you picked.
  3. Ask for one answer per household. Say so in the details: "One reply per household, please, under a name like The Okafors". A poll holds up to 60 rows, which is plenty for households even when the family is far bigger than that.
  4. Put the rough plan in the details: the area, whether people will need somewhere to stay, and who to ask about it.
  5. Set a voting deadline that leaves time to book. The poll closes itself and tells everyone when that is.

Getting answers from every branch

There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for. Relatives open the link, type a name and tap yes or no on each weekend, with big buttons that work on any phone. Text the link, email it, or post it in the family group chat; in most chat apps it shows the poll's title. Switch on "maybe" so the cousin waiting on a shift schedule can say so instead of guessing.

Reading a two day weekend

Each date is answered on its own. For a Saturday and Sunday reunion, look for a weekend where both columns score well. The tally under each column shows that at a glance, and the highlighted best date points at the strongest single day.

Keep the organizer link safe

You will come back to this poll over weeks or months, maybe from a different device. Save the organizer link when you create the poll: download it, email it to yourself, or sign in so the poll is kept on your account and reachable from anywhere.

Questions

Grandma does not have a smartphone. Can someone answer for her?

Yes. Anyone can add a row under her name from their own phone. Only the browser that added a row can change it later, and the organizer can remove a duplicate row.

Can relatives see each other's answers?

Yes. Anyone with the link sees every name and answer, so the whole family can see which weekends are filling up.
