What people use Scheduler for

Every group has its own version of the same problem: some dates, some people, and no easy way to see which date suits most of them. These pages show how to set up a poll for a particular kind of group, using the features that matter for it.

Create a free poll No account needed, no ads.

Use cases

  • D&D session scheduler: Find an evening the DM and enough players can make, session after session.
  • Book club date poll: Pick next month's meeting with time left to finish the book.
  • Family reunion date poll: One answer per household, weekends offered months ahead.
  • Class reunion date poll: A long lead time, a scattered class, and nobody making an account.
  • Team offsite date poll: Spot the run of days where the whole team is free.
  • Band practice scheduler: Evening slots around day jobs and rehearsal room bookings.
  • Volunteer meeting poll: Mixed availability, and people who will not sign up for anything.
  • Bachelor party date poll: Fridays and Saturdays months out, shared only with the group.
  • Group date poll: Dinners, trips and birthdays with friends.
  • Meeting poll: Time slots for people who do not share a calendar, in every time zone.

Compare

How Scheduler stacks up against other scheduling tools, including when the other tool is the better pick.

  • Scheduler and Doodle: No organizer account and no ads for a group date poll.
  • Scheduler and When2meet: Tap yes or no instead of dragging over a grid.
  • Scheduler and Framadate: No email address needed to make a poll.
  • Scheduler and Rallly: No limit on open polls, against email updates and self-hosting.
  • Scheduler and StrawPoll: A tool that only does dates, with no ads.
  • Scheduler and Crab Fit: A yes or no shortlist against a heat map of free time.
