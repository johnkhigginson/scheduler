Scheduler and Rallly, two free date polls compared

Rallly is the tool closest to Scheduler. Both are free and open source, and nobody needs an account to create a poll or to answer one. Both show times on each voter's own clock. The differences are in what happens around the poll, so here they are side by side.

What you get with Scheduler

  • No cap on how many polls you have open. Run one for the team and one for the weekend at the same time without closing either.
  • Yes or no answers by default, with "maybe" as an option you can switch on or off at any time, even after people have answered.
  • Time slots built from opening hours: say 9 to 5 in one hour slots, with different hours on Saturdays.
  • A one line shortcut for voters, such as "set yes for every Tuesday at 7 PM", for polls with many slots.
  • Comments on every poll, which the person who wrote them can edit later.
  • Nothing is ever emailed to voters. The only emails Scheduler sends are a sign in link and, if you ask for it, your own poll links.

When Rallly is the better choice

Rallly does more around the poll. If you want an email each time someone responds, or want to finalize the date and have calendar invites sent to every participant for you, Rallly does both and Scheduler does neither. It is also the one to pick if you want to run the software on your own server, or if more than 60 people need to answer a single poll.

Scheduler suits the organizer who wants several polls running at once, no limits to keep track of, and nothing sent to anyone's inbox.
