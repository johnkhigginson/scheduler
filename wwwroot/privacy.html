Privacy

Last updated: October 25, 2026

Scheduler is a small personal project by John Higginson. It is built to collect as little as possible. This page lists everything it keeps and why. The code is open source, so you can check every line of this against it.

The short version

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking pixels, no third-party scripts or fonts. Every file the site loads comes from scheduler.sdw.dev.
  • You do not need an account to create a poll or to vote.
  • Nothing is sold or shared. The only outside service that sees any of your data is Postmark, and only when Scheduler sends you an email.

What a poll stores

When you create a poll, the database stores the title, the details and your name if you give them, the dates and times, whether "maybe" is allowed, the deadline if you set one, when the poll was created, and a secret organizer token (the part after #a= in your organizer link). It also stores the time zone your browser reports, such as America/Denver, so voters know which clock the times refer to.

When someone votes, the poll stores the name they type, their answers, when the row was added and last changed, and a secret edit token that lets their browser change the row later. A comment stores the name, the text, when it was posted, and an edit token.

Polls are private by link, not by password. Anyone with a poll's link can see its title, details, organizer name, dates, every voter's name and answers, and the comments. Do not put anything in a poll that you would not want everyone with the link to read.

Accounts, which are optional

If you sign in, Scheduler stores your email address, when the account was created and when it was last used. Polls you create while signed in, or that are added to your account after you sign in, record that email address as their owner so they appear on your My polls page.

Sign in links and sessions are stored only as SHA-256 hashes, never as the values themselves. A sign in link expires after 15 minutes and is deleted once used. A session lasts 30 days. Expired links and sessions are deleted automatically, and signing out deletes that session.

Email

Scheduler sends email through Postmark. When you ask for a sign in link, your email address and the link are sent to Postmark so it can deliver the message. When you use "Email me both links" after creating a poll, your email address, the poll title and both links are sent to Postmark the same way. Scheduler does not store that address, and does not write it to its logs. Postmark keeps its own delivery records under its own privacy policy. Scheduler sends no newsletters and no marketing email.

Cookies and browser storage

There is one cookie, sid, and it is only set when you sign in. It holds your session, cannot be read by page scripts, and expires after 30 days. If you never sign in, Scheduler sets no cookies at all.

Your browser's local storage holds the secrets that let you edit things without an account. They stay on your device, and are only sent back to Scheduler to prove you may make a change:

  • admin: followed by a poll id: the organizer token for a poll you created, or opened with its organizer link.
  • poll: followed by a poll id: that poll's title and when it was created, so the home page can list "Your polls on this device".
  • me: followed by a poll id: which row in that poll is yours, and its edit token.
  • comments: followed by a poll id: edit tokens for your comments.
  • site-admin-key: only on the site's own admin page, and only once the operator unlocks it.

Clearing your browser's site data removes all of these. After that you can no longer edit your own rows or comments from that browser, and the organizer link is gone unless you saved it somewhere else.

IP addresses and logs

To limit abuse, the server counts requests that change something (creating a poll, voting, commenting, asking for a sign in link) per IP address. Those counts are kept in memory only, last an hour at most, and are never written to the database. Scheduler does not keep access logs of visits. Its error log can include an email address when an email fails to send. That log stays on the server and is only used to fix problems.

Who can see what

  • Anyone with the poll link can see the poll, as described above.
  • Anyone with the organizer link can edit, close or delete the poll and remove any row or comment in it.
  • The site operator can see every poll on the server, including organizer links, through a private admin page. That is used to hand back lost organizer links and to remove abuse.

Where it lives

The app runs on a server we operate. The database is hosted by MongoDB Atlas.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

Polls do not expire on their own at the moment. A poll stays until its organizer deletes it, which removes the poll and every vote and comment in it at once. Voters can delete their own row, and their own comments, from the browser they used.

There is no button to delete an account yet. To have your account and its email address removed, or to ask about a poll you cannot reach, open an issue on GitHub. Do not post your email address in the issue. We will reply there with a private way to confirm it is you.

Open source

Scheduler is open source under the MIT license. The full source is on GitHub.

Changes and questions
