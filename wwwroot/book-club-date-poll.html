A date poll for your book club's next meeting

Most book clubs meet about once a month, and the date has two jobs. It has to suit whoever is hosting, and it has to leave everyone enough time to finish the book. Ask in the group chat and you get eight replies about the date and three about the ending.

Set it up

  1. Look three to five weeks out. Click the evenings you would consider on the calendar, or shift click to add a run of days. If your group sometimes meets on a weekend afternoon, add those days too.
  2. Leave the time as all day if your club always starts at the same hour, and put it in the details: "7:30, at Dana's this month". Offer time slots only if the start time is genuinely up for discussion.
  3. Name the book in the details. A member opening the poll a week later is reminded what they are meant to be reading.
  4. Switch on maybe. "I will come if I have finished it" is an honest answer, and it scores half a yes.

Use the comments

Every poll has a comment thread. It is a good place for "I am only a third of the way in, later is better for me", or for suggesting next month's book while everyone is looking. Whoever posts a comment can edit it later from the same browser.

Picking the evening

The date with the most yes answers highlights itself. If two evenings tie, the host's row is the tiebreaker that matters. Close voting from the organizer link so the poll shows it is settled, then share the calendar file from "Add to calendar" with the group.

If hosting rotates, the host can make each month's poll themselves. It takes a minute and needs no account. Whoever makes it should keep the organizer link: copy it, download it, or email it to themselves from the page that appears after creating the poll.

Questions

Can members see who else is coming?

Yes. Everyone with the link sees every name and every answer, which is usually what a book club wants. It also means the details box is no place for anything private.

Some members never answer polls. What then?

Set a voting deadline when you create the poll. It closes on its own at that time, and the page shows everyone how long they have left.
